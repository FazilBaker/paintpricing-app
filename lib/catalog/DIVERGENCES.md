# Catalog reconciliation (P0)

Before `catalog.json` existed, the free calculator in the WordPress theme
(`page-calculator.php`) and the app's quote engine (`lib/quote-engine.ts` +
`lib/constants.ts`) held two independent sets of pricing coefficients. They had
drifted apart by as much as 8x.

This is a record of every conflict and how it was resolved. **Both engines are
now wired to the catalog** and produce identical prices for an identical job.

- App: `lib/constants.ts` derives from the catalog, `lib/quote-engine.ts` uses it.
- Theme: `page-calculator.php` reads `window.PP_CATALOG`, enqueued in the head by
  `paintpricing_scripts()` and generated by `npm run build:catalog`.

Run `npm run build:catalog` after every catalog edit or the two will drift again.

**Neither side was a superset of the other.**

- The app was better on exteriors: per surface coverage and separate brush and
  spray production rates.
- The theme was better on the cost stack: paint grades, supplies, surface
  condition, a display range, and more room presets.

The catalog takes the better half of each.

## Labor

| Coefficient | Theme | App | Resolved | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Door labor | 0.5 hr per door **per coat** (1.0 hr at 2 coats) | `doorCount / 8` = 0.125 hr, coat independent | **0.5 hr per door per coat** | The app value implies 8 doors per hour. A door painted both faces with prep does not happen in 7.5 minutes. This is the largest single error in either engine. |
| Window labor | 0.35 hr per window per coat (0.7 at 2 coats) | `windowCount / 10` = 0.1 hr | **0.35 hr per window per coat** | Same reasoning. 10 windows per hour is not achievable. |
| Hourly rate | 45 | 45 | 45 | Agreed. |
| Wall production | 200 sq ft/hr | 200 sq ft/hr | 200 | Agreed. |
| Trim production | 100 lin ft/hr | 100 lin ft/hr | 100 | Agreed. |
| Ceiling production | not separated | not separated | **175 sq ft/hr** | New. Both engines priced ceilings at the wall rate. Overhead rolling is slower than wall rolling. |
| Cleanup | 0.5 base + 0.1 per item | 0.5 flat per quote | **0.5 base + 0.1 per item** | The theme version scales with job size, which is correct. |
| Spray | global 1.8x multiplier | per surface spray rates | **per surface, 1.8x as fallback** | The app's approach is better. The multiplier survives only for surfaces that declare no spray rate. |

## Room geometry

The app carried flat wall and ceiling allowances that were not derived from any
room dimensions. The theme derived them from length, width and height. The
catalog stores dimensions and derives, because the derived figure is simply
correct: a 13 by 11 by 9 bedroom has 432 sq ft of wall, not 320.

| Room | App wall | Derived wall | App ceiling | Derived ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Living room | 420 | 540 | 240 | 224 |
| Master bedroom | 380 | 504 | 210 | 195 |
| Standard bedroom | 320 | 432 | 160 | 143 |
| Kitchen | 280 | 432 | 120 | 0 (excluded) |
| Bathroom | 180 | 256 | 64 | 63 |
| Hallway | 240 | 414 | 90 | 90 |

The app was understating wall area on every room template, by 26% on a bedroom
and 54% on a kitchen. The free calculator has been showing the derived figure to
the public the whole time.

## Structural divergences found during wiring

Matching the coefficients closed only part of the gap. On an identical job the
two engines were still 20.4% apart, because they disagreed about the *shape* of
the calculation, not just its inputs. Four bugs, all now fixed.

| # | Divergence | Which side was wrong | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Labor did not scale with coat count.** The app charged the same labor for a three coat job as a one coat job. The theme scaled correctly. | App | App now multiplies wall, ceiling, trim and exterior labor by coats. |
| 2 | **Doors and windows were painted but not paid for in materials.** The theme charged labor for door and window faces and never added their paint. | Theme | Theme now adds door and window face area to the trim gallons. |
| 3 | **Gallon rounding differed.** The app rounded wall and trim gallons separately to the quarter gallon; the theme summed both and rounded to a tenth. Wall paint and trim paint are separate products bought separately, so rounding each is correct. | Theme | Theme now rounds each bucket to the quarter gallon. |
| 4 | **Prep used different areas.** The app prepped gross wall area and ignored the ceiling. The theme prepped paintable wall plus ceiling, which matches what actually gets prepped. | App | App now preps paintable wall plus ceiling. |

Cleanup was also missing from the app's current path entirely. Per item cleanup
now prices inside each item, and the flat per job base is added once in
`calculateItemsSummary` rather than multiplied across every item.

**Result: the two engines now agree to 0.000% on every room preset.** A painter
who prices a job on the public calculator and then rebuilds it in the product
gets the same number.

## Price impact on new app quotes

Existing saved quotes are unaffected. They store their own inputs, and the
deprecated `calculateQuoteSummary` path is frozen (see `LEGACY_ASSUMPTIONS`).
This changes what new quotes suggest.

| Room | Without door and window work | With |
|---|---|---|
| Living room | $918 | $1,149 |
| Dining room | $799 | $938 |
| Master bedroom | $850 | $1,048 |
| Bedroom | $727 | $866 |
| Kitchen | $578 | $670 |
| Bathroom | $449 | $509 |
| Hallway | $666 | $831 |
| Home office | $696 | $788 |
| Laundry room | $449 | $509 |

A standard bedroom quoted at **$417** before this work now quotes at **$727**,
or **$866** with the door and two windows painted.

That is a large increase and it deserves scrutiny. It is the sum of five
corrections, every one of which moved in the same direction because the app was
understating in five independent places:

1. Room geometry derived rather than allowanced (a bedroom has 432 sq ft of wall, not 320)
2. Labor scaled by coat count (a two coat job takes about twice the rolling time of one)
3. Door and window labor corrected from roughly 8x too fast
4. Prep extended to cover the ceiling
5. Supplies and cleanup added, both previously missing

Partly offsetting: paint dropped from a flat $50 to $45 standard grade, and
material markup from 40% to 35%.

For a sanity check, $727 for a fully painted bedroom including ceiling, 44 linear
feet of trim, two coats and prep is roughly 12 labor hours plus materials. That
is a normal number for the trade. $417 was not.

## Open questions for review

1. **Door and window labor is the one call worth challenging.** It raises app
   prices on trim heavy jobs. It is also the correction that makes the app agree
   with the free calculator, and the current app figure is not defensible on its
   own terms. Confirm before wiring.

2. **Minimum job charge defaults to 0 in both engines.** Every working painting
   business has one, typically 350 to 500 for interior work. A default of 0 lets
   the calculator return a number no contractor would ever accept. Recommend
   setting a real default, but this is a product decision, not a reconciliation
   one.

3. **The resolved exterior production rates are trade reasoning, not measured
   data.** They sit between the two prior values and are defensible, but the only
   way to make them right is to compare against jobs the user has actually
   priced. Worth revisiting once real painters are using the tool.

4. **Material markup at 35% recovers overhead on the smallest line in the job.**
   This is the L7 gap from the atlas and it is not fixed here. P0 only makes the
   two engines agree; it does not make the pricing model correct.
